# Can we predict attacks?

**TLDR — how we predict attacks:** an attack fires the moment a faction's
campaign hits full points — `points == points_max`, exactly, within minutes.
So the forecast is just arithmetic:
`eta = (points_max − points) / rate`, with a day-of-week correction to the
pace. Backtested walk-forward it clears every leg of the project's
pre-registered gate — skill 0.23–0.33 against a 0.6 bar — and ships on the
dashboard as an assault heads-up line. It never becomes a clock: the window
is ~21h wide a day out, ~5h wide two hours out. <a href="/docs/predict/defend" data-umami-event="docs-predict-defend">How the defend side
compares →</a>

Attacks are **deterministic**. An attack on a faction's homeworld fires within minutes of that faction's campaign reaching full points — `points == points_max`, the exact integer. Every one of the 925 recorded attacks targets region 11, the enemy homeworld; none fires early.

There is no threshold to estimate and no distribution to model. The trigger is a constant already stored in `h1_season.points_max`.

### The trigger is a point, not a band

Read at daily resolution, attack starts *appear* spread across a band of liberation values — a median near 96%, most firing somewhere above 90%. That spread belongs to the sensor, not the game: `h1_status` runs at roughly one bucket per day for 156 of the 160 seasons, so the campaign state read at an attack's start can be up to 24 hours stale, and a hard threshold viewed through a lagging sensor smears downward. Sorting the same 925 attacks by how fresh the reading was makes it plain:

| Age of the campaign reading | Attacks | Median liberation at attack start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| &lt;15 min | 13 | **100.00%** |
| &lt;1h | 23 | 99.69% |
| &lt;6h | 170 | 98.87% |
| &lt;12h | 182 | 97.37% |
| &lt;24h | 537 | 94.28% |

The "band" was a staleness gradient. Read fresh, the trigger is a point.

The thirteen freshest readings are more convincing individually than any median, because the residual shortfalls scale with how early the reading was taken:

| Season | Enemy | Reading age | Points / max | Short by | Liberation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | 2 | 723s | 1,018,465 / 1,020,040 | 1,575 | 99.846% |
| 20 | 0 | **62s** | 763,194 / 763,240 | **46** | **99.994%** |
| 30 | 1 | 421s | 547,798 / 548,210 | 412 | 99.925% |
| 60 | 1 | 482s | 636,241 / 636,530 | 289 | 99.955% |
| 106 | 2 | 781s | 325,657 / 325,740 | 83 | 99.975% |
| 143 | 2 | 361s | 253,842 / 253,910 | 68 | 99.973% |
| 158 | 0 | 841s | 356,240 / 356,240 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 158 | 1 | 121s | 275,790 / 275,790 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 158 | 2 | 421s | 147,470 / 147,470 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 158 | 2 | 541s | 147,470 / 147,470 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 159 | 0 | 121s | 280,970 / 280,970 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 159 | 1 | 61s | 325,480 / 325,480 | **0** | **100.000%** |
| 159 | 2 | 182s | 202,300 / 202,300 | **0** | **100.000%** |

Twelve of thirteen sit within 0.1% of full. Season 20's Bug campaign was **46 points short of 763,240 sixty-two seconds before the attack began** — a sensor catching the last seconds of a climb, not a threshold with slack in it. The six sub-100% rows are all pre-S157 seasons whose whole campaign is covered by 13–43 buckets; their shortfall tracks their reading age, exactly as a hard threshold predicts.

### How soon after reaching full points?

For the four seasons with 15-minute resolution, the gap between the first bucket at full points and the attack's start is **1 to 14 minutes, median 3.0** (n = 7). Since a bucket can be up to 15 minutes wide, these are upper bounds. The trigger is effectively immediate.

### Can we forecast the next one?

With the trigger known exactly, forecasting an attack stops being a question about the trigger and becomes a question about **pace**:

```
eta = (points_max − points) / rate
```

Everything on the right-hand side except `rate` is known exactly and in real time. So the entire forecasting problem — and the entire error budget — lives in estimating how fast a campaign is gaining points.

**Measured, and it works.** `10-attack-eta.mjs` backtests that formula walk-forward by season against all 925 attacks, using a 24-hour pace window:

| Faction | Effective N | Skill ratio [95% CI] | Median error vs. baseline | Sharpness vs. marginal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bugs | 94 | **0.270** [0.203, 0.356] | 17.3h vs. 64.0h | 69.6h vs. 90.4h — narrower |
| Cyborgs | 96 | **0.233** [0.186, 0.311] | 18.9h vs. 81.3h | 85.5h vs. 94.0h — narrower |
| Illuminate | 125 | **0.325** [0.246, 0.392] | 19.9h vs. 61.3h | 69.9h vs. 88.4h — narrower |

Every confidence interval sits far below the project's 0.6 ship bar, and every configuration passes the sharpness leg. For scale: the best defend model ever built reached 0.679 [0.651, 0.707] and failed. **This is the first thing in this project to clear the gate it was measured against**, and it does so because the trigger is exact — the model is only forecasting pace, not guessing at a threshold.

The forecast is not available at every moment. Roughly **38–47%** of moments emit nothing at all, because a stalled or retreating front (`rate <= 0`) gives no arrival time. A front that is not moving has no ETA, and the honest output is silence.

### What a player would actually see

The intended use is a heads-up, not a countdown, so it is scored as an alert. Both bars were written down before the model was run:

| Faction | Fires before…​ | …​an attack follows within 2× the upper bound | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bugs | 93.6% of attacks | 92.0% of showing moments | PASS |
| Cyborgs | 96.9% | 97.4% | PASS |
| Illuminate | 96.8% | 95.7% | PASS |

Against bars of 70% and 80% respectively, with the line rendering when the median estimate falls under **48 hours**.

**Why 48 and not 24.** The threshold was 24h first, and moving it out improved every measure at once — coverage, false-alarm rate, and calibration. That is the campaign-state resolution showing through: `h1_status` runs at ~1 bucket/day for most of the record, so a 24-hour display window is exactly the regime where the input is least able to support an estimate. Restricted to the moments a UI renders, the median hit rate moved from 0.456–0.526 to **0.496–0.535** against a nominal 0.500 — Cyborgs at 0.496 is very nearly exact. A wider window also means the line appears more often and is correspondingly less of an event; 48h is the point where the data can carry the claim. And the window does tighten as the attack approaches — this is the model's own output, on the moments a UI would render:

| When it says | It shows | Width | The attack actually came |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ~2h | 0.8–5.6h | 4.8h | 2.5h |
| ~6h | 4.3–12.6h | 8.3h | 6.0h |
| ~10h | 7.8–19.2h | 11.5h | 9.2h |
| ~14h | 10.7–25.0h | 14.3h | 12.9h |
| ~18h | 13.8–31.4h | 17.7h | 16.0h |
| ~22h | 17.2–38.3h | 21.1h | 20.8h |

A day out the window is ~21 hours wide; a couple of hours out it is ~5. **It never becomes a clock.** The width tracks the estimate itself, so the display goes `17–38h` → `8–19h` → `1–6h` and stops. Curiously the *relative* uncertainty is slightly worse close in — roughly 1× the estimate a day out, 2× a couple of hours out — which is the daily-bucket staleness biting hardest when little remains to conquer.

### While the assault runs

Once the assault fires, the question flips from *when* to *how it ends*, and
the event card switches to the [outcome verdict](/docs/predict): at the
average pace since the assault started, does its bar fill inside the 48-hour
timeout? On track → _"Taken ~12h"_; behind → _"Fails"_ (the countdown already
shows the loss time).

The verdict is unusually decisive on assaults. Conditioning the outcome on
the live pace (script 17, S157+ progress history, 8 assaults so far):

| Elapsed | P(fail) if on track | P(fail) if behind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10–35% | 0.000 (n=77) | 0.672 (n=58) |
| 35–60% | 0.000 (n=149) | 0.652 (n=198) |
| 60–85% | 0.000 (n=77) | 1.000 (n=144) |
| 85–100% | 0.000 (n=20) | 1.000 (n=87) |

**No on-track moment has ever preceded a failed assault** — in any elapsed
bin — while a behind reading fails about two-thirds of the time early and
always past 60% elapsed. Read direction, not precision: the moment counts
are autocorrelated within events and only 8 assaults have progress history.
Quantifying this into the counterattack clock is
[#487](https://github.com/elfensky/helldivers.bot/issues/487), blocked until
~30 assaults have progress capture.

## The sector ETA — the same arithmetic, one boundary early

The faction cards' sector view shows an `ETA ~9h` line toward the **next
sector boundary** — the same `remaining ÷ pace` computation as the assault
ETA (literally the same code path, `paceEtaHours` in
`attackForecast.mjs`), aimed at the next `points_max / 10` step instead of
the campaign's end. Same 24-hour rate window, same day-of-week correction,
same staleness anchoring.

Two honest differences from the assault window:

- **Median only, no range.** The band ratios that turn the assault estimate
  into a calibrated `(10–25h)` window were measured against one target —
  full campaign completion. No equivalent table exists yet for sector
  boundaries (the grading script needs more high-resolution seasons,
  [#484](https://github.com/elfensky/helldivers.bot/issues/484)), and an
  unmeasured range is not shown.
- **Hidden during any active event for that faction.** Defends freeze the
  campaign bar; during an assault the campaign is already complete.

In the **last sector** the next boundary *is* the campaign end — exactly the
target the calibrated assault model was measured on — so the card defers to
the full assault window rather than showing an uncalibrated median for the
same number.

## Why the window is lopsided

The dashboard shows the forecast as `ETA ~14h (10–25h)` — a median with an
*asymmetric* range, never a symmetric `±`. That is not a style choice. The
skew is baked into the arithmetic, twice over.

**Division skews time.** The forecast is `arrival = points remaining ÷ pace`,
and division treats the two directions unequally. Pace swings are symmetric —
players arrive, players leave — but their effect on the arrival time is not:

| Pace change | Arrival | Shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ×2 (doubles) | 7h | 7h sooner |
| ×1 (as forecast) | 14h | — |
| ×½ (halves) | 28h | **14h later** |
| ×¼ | 56h | **42h later** |

Speeding up can at best push the arrival toward zero — it can never save more
than the hours already on the clock. Slowing down has no such limit: as pace
approaches zero, the arrival time grows without bound. Drag the pace and
watch the curve do it:

**Surges are capped, stalls are not.** The second half of the skew is
supply. For an attack to land much *earlier* than forecast, extra players
must show up — and a front can realistically double its pace on a good
evening, rarely more. For it to land much *later*, players only have to
*leave*: sleep, weekdays, a juicier event on another front. Showing up has a
supply limit; leaving does not.

The measured history agrees: across 160 seasons, when the raw arithmetic said
"X hours", reality typically landed between ~0.85×X and ~2.3×X depending on
how close the campaign was to completion — small early misses, large late
ones. A `±` label would promise a symmetry the game has never once delivered;
the `(10–25h)` range is the same information, told honestly.

<summary>In depth: the pace estimate, its corrections, and what carries no signal</summary>

**Staleness anchoring.** `points` is read from the last bucket at or before the evaluation moment — up to 24 hours old in the historical record — so raw `remaining / rate` answers *"how long from when the reading was taken"*, not *"how long from now."* The correction is algebraic: extrapolating the stale reading forward at the same rate is identical to subtracting its age.

```
(max − points − rate × age) / rate  ==  (max − points) / rate − age
```

Median historical reading age is ~12h, so the anchor matters most exactly in the sub-24h display regime. The when-showing p50 hit rate, against a nominal 0.500:

| p50 hit rate when showing (nominal 0.500) | Unanchored | Anchored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bugs | 0.650 | 0.525 |
| Cyborgs | 0.678 | 0.544 |
| Illuminate | 0.701 | 0.568 |

One methodological note worth keeping: the headline calibration figure pools every moment, so a model can score well on it while being miscalibrated in every stratum with the errors cancelling. Calibration is therefore also checked by predicted-magnitude decile, which is what makes anchoring effects like this visible at all.

**The rate window was chosen empirically, without using a single attack.** "Which window best predicts near-future pace" is answerable on all 21,849 high-resolution status rows, which sidesteps the fact that only 8 attacks have ever been recorded at 15-minute resolution. Median error predicting the next six hours of pace:

| Window | 30 min | 1h | 3h | 6h | 12h | **24h** | 48h | 72h |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Error | 42.8% | 34.4% | 28.7% | 28.9% | 29.4% | **23.7%** | 24.5% | 24.5% |

Short windows are far worse, not better — at 15-minute buckets a 30-minute rate is a difference of two readings and mostly noise. Longer than a day is no better either. Blending a short window into the long one helps by 0.6pp at a 20% weight, which does not justify the complexity.

**Adjustments that carry no signal.** Two telemetry-based adjustments from `h1_statistic` test *worse* than the plain 24-hour rate (23.5% pace error). The points-rate over a window already embodies the players who produced it, so layering a player multiplier on top double-counts:

| Adjustment | Error |
| --- | --- |
| × (current players / 24h average players) | 28.1% |
| points-per-mission × recent mission rate | 27.0% |

**Per-faction player telemetry — promising, unproven.** Four seasons of 15-minute per-faction player counts exist (S157–160), enough for a walk-forward comparison of player-based pace variants against the shipped model, every variant scored on identical moments. The facts: the crude ratio above tests worse here too (+2.4h median paired error, pooled); a dampened points-per-player<sup>α</sup>-hour form at α = 0.7 tests *better* (−1.0h pooled, better in both Cyborg seasons). The doubt: only 2–3 scorable attacks per faction exist at this resolution, far too few to conclude anything. The shipped model stands; the comparison reruns unchanged as each new war adds a telemetry season.

**The day-of-week correction.** Campaign pace has a strong weekly rhythm — across all 160 seasons, normalising each campaign to its own median pace:

| Sun | Mon | Sat | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.19 | 1.19 | 1.00 | 0.95 | 0.93 | 0.92 | 0.92 |

A 29% swing between the busiest and quietest days. (Sunday and Monday lead because a daily bucket reports the day before it, so weekend play lands there.) In-sample, an hour-of-week correction cuts the *pace* error from 23.5% to **18.6%** — a figure to distrust on principle, since it is learned on the same data it is scored against.

Measured honestly — factors learned from training seasons only, day rather than hour because for 156 of 160 seasons a status transition spans a whole day — the skill ratio is a wash: Bugs 0.270 → 0.275, Cyborgs 0.233 → 0.232, Illuminate 0.325 → 0.271, with heavily overlapping intervals. Better pace prediction does not translate into a better median ETA.

What the correction *does* improve is calibration in the regime a UI renders — the numbers a player actually reads:

| When showing (nominal p25 0.250 / p50 0.500) | Without | With day-of-week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bugs | 0.294 / 0.553 | **0.219 / 0.507** |
| Cyborgs | 0.297 / 0.538 | **0.251 / 0.496** |
| Illuminate | 0.318 / 0.579 | **0.276 / 0.535** |

So the correction makes the displayed window more *honest* without making the central estimate more *accurate*. For a heads-up, that is the trade worth taking, and it is kept. Coverage improves too — the line fires before 93.6–96.0% of attacks.

Public holidays were not tested and are not worth testing: the game's history contains a handful of them.

**Caveats.**

- **Effective N is 94–125 per faction**, not 925. Attacks average 2.17 per faction-season, so most clock moments share a target.
- **The calibration leg flips between factions** (two of three pass, and which two depends on configuration). At this effective N that is noise, not signal, and it should not be read as a result either way.
- **The live model is better fed than the backtested one.** Historical campaign state is up to 24 hours stale; live it is 15 minutes. The same feature measured with less noise makes the backtested error a floor rather than an estimate — but with 8 high-resolution attacks that cannot be demonstrated, only argued.
